# Common Screening Questions

This page records agreed answers to common eligibility questions raised by reviewers during and after the screening workshop.

Please apply these clarifications together with the full inclusion and exclusion criteria. A paper must meet all other relevant eligibility criteria to be included.

---

## 1. The paper reports deaths but does not report DALYs or YLLs

### Case

The paper meets the other eligibility criteria and reports death or mortality as an outcome, but it does not provide information on disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) or years of life lost (YLLs).

### Decision: INCLUDE

### Clarification

Papers do not need to report only DALYs or YLLs to be eligible. These were provided as examples of mortality-related measures and are not mandatory outcomes.

A paper can be included if it reports a relevant measure of death or mortality, such as:

- Number of deaths
- Mortality rate
- Mortality risk
- Case-fatality ratio
- Proportion of participants who died
- Survival
- Excess mortality
- Estimated mortality burden

The paper must also meet all remaining eligibility criteria.

---

## 2. Are fetal loss and stillbirth considered deaths?

### Case

The paper reports fetal loss, fetal death, intrauterine fetal death or stillbirth as an outcome and meets the other eligibility criteria.

### Decision: INCLUDE

### Clarification

For the purposes of this review, **fetal loss and stillbirth are considered mortality-related outcomes**.

The paper should therefore be included when it reports or analyses outcomes such as:

- Fetal loss
- Fetal death
- Intrauterine fetal death
- Stillbirth
- Stillbirth rate
- Number or proportion of pregnancies resulting in fetal death or stillbirth

As with all records, the paper must also meet the remaining geographical, study-period, and HRP eligibility criteria.

---

## 3. The relevant information is only reported in the supplementary material

### Case

The relevant mortality data are reported in an official supplementary appendix, table, figure or file rather than in the main manuscript.

### Decision: INCLUDE

### Clarification

Supplementary materials are considered part of the publication.

If an eligible mortality outcome is reported in the supplementary material, the paper should be included, provided that it also meets the remaining eligibility criteria.

Reviewers should check supplementary files where they are available and note in the screening comments that the relevant information was found in the supplementary material.

However, the paper should not be included if the supplementary material only quotes mortality estimates from another source and does not report or analyse mortality findings from the study itself.

---

## 4. The paper only quotes mortality statistics from the Global Burden of Disease study or another source

### Case

The paper quotes or reproduces mortality statistics from the Global Burden of Disease study, WHO or another external source, but does not conduct its own analysis or report study findings relating to mortality.

### Decision: EXCLUDE

### Clarification

Mortality figures presented only as background information or copied from another source are not sufficient for inclusion.

The paper should be excluded when it only:

- Quotes previously published mortality estimates
- Reproduces figures from the Global Burden of Disease study
- Mentions mortality statistics in the introduction or discussion
- Uses mortality figures only to provide context for another topic

However, if the paper uses an existing empirical dataset to conduct a new analysis and reports its own mortality findings or estimates, it should not be automatically excluded. Assess it against the remaining eligibility criteria and flag it for discussion if the decision remains uncertain.

---

## 5. The paper is a systematic review

### Case

The paper is a systematic review that reports or synthesises mortality evidence from one or more eligible HRP countries.

### Decision: INCLUDE AND TAG SEPARATELY

### Clarification

Systematic reviews are eligible when they report or synthesise relevant mortality evidence for one or more eligible countries.

Tag the study separately in the screening sheet so that it can be identified during subsequent analysis.

The review must still meet the geographical, study-period and mortality-outcome criteria.

---

## 6. The paper is a modelling study

### Case

The paper uses a model to estimate or analyse mortality outcomes.

### Decision: DEPENDS ON THE DATA USED

### Clarification

Include the study if its mortality findings are based on empirical data.

Exclude studies that are based entirely on:

- Simulated data
- Hypothetical scenarios
- Projection-only analyses
- Future forecasts without empirical mortality data

Flag the paper for discussion if it is unclear whether the model is informed by empirical data.

---

## 7. The paper reports global or multi-country findings

### Case

The study reports mortality outcomes across multiple countries or at the global or regional level.

### Decision: INCLUDE ONLY IF COUNTRY-SPECIFIC RESULTS ARE REPORTED

### Clarification

The study is eligible only when mortality findings are reported separately for one or more eligible HRP countries.

Exclude the paper when mortality results are presented only as a combined global, regional or multi-country estimate and cannot be attributed to a specific eligible country or a more granular geographical unit.

---

## 8. The paper includes data collection periods spanning before and during/after the first HRP year

### Case

The paper reports data from multiple separate surveys or data collection periods, or from a single data collection period that spans both before and during/after the country’s first HRP year.

**Decision: INCLUDE only if mortality data from the eligible period can be identified separately.**

### Clarification

The paper can be included if the data collected during or after the country’s first HRP year report an eligible mortality-related outcome and the paper meets all other eligibility criteria.

If the study includes data from both before and during/after the first HRP year, the mortality data for the eligible period must be **reported separately**, either by year or by clearly defined time periods.

For example, if a study collected data from **2014–2018** and the country’s first HRP year was **2018**:

- If mortality outcomes are reported separately for each year, including 2018 → **INCLUDE**. Only the eligible 2018 data should be considered.
- If the paper reports only one overall mortality estimate for the entire 2014–2018 period, with no way to separate the 2018 mortality data from the earlier years → **EXCLUDE**.
- If the study divides the data into distinct periods (e.g., pre/post an event) and mortality outcomes for a period occurring during or after the first HRP year are reported separately → **INCLUDE**.

Data collected before the first HRP year would not be considered eligible for the review.

During screening, please add a note indicating which part of the study period meets the HRP timing criterion. This will help ensure that only the eligible data collection period(s) are considered during data extraction.

## 9. The article is not peer-reviewed or is a conference abstract

### Case
The record is a conference abstract, conference proceeding, poster abstract, preprint, commentary, editorial, letter, or other publication that has not undergone peer review.

### Decision: EXCLUDE

### Clarification
Only peer-reviewed research articles are eligible for inclusion in this review.

Conference abstracts and other non-peer-reviewed publications should therefore be excluded, even if they appear to report relevant mortality outcomes or otherwise meet the eligibility criteria.

If a conference abstract or non-peer-reviewed record appears to correspond to a later peer-reviewed full-text publication, reviewers should exclude the abstract/non-peer-reviewed record and screen the peer-reviewed publication separately if it is available in the dataset.

If you are unsure whether a publication has been peer-reviewed, flag the record for discussion and contact us.

---

## 10. The study population was displaced from an HRP country into a country without an HRP

### Case

The study reports mortality outcomes for refugees, asylum seekers, migrants or other cross-border displaced populations whose country of origin had a Humanitarian Response Plan, but the mortality data were collected or reported in a host country that did not have an HRP.

### Decision: EXCLUDE unless eligible-country mortality data are reported separately

### Clarification

HRP eligibility should be based on the country or territory where the mortality data or deaths are attributable, not on nationality or country of origin alone.

Include the study only when mortality outcomes can be attributed separately to an eligible HRP country during the eligible period. For example:

- Deaths among internally displaced persons within an eligible HRP country -> **INCLUDE**, if all other criteria are met.
- A study of refugees from an HRP country after arrival in a non-HRP host country, with mortality outcomes reported only for the host-country setting -> **EXCLUDE**.
- A mixed study of displaced people in several host countries -> **INCLUDE only** if mortality outcomes are reported separately for one or more eligible HRP countries or deaths occurring in those eligible countries.
- A study reporting deaths during flight, transit or displacement from an HRP country -> **INCLUDE only** if the deaths can be attributed to the eligible HRP country and eligible period; otherwise flag for discussion.

If the paper reports only that participants came from an HRP country, but the deaths or mortality outcomes occurred in and are attributable to a non-HRP host country, origin-country HRP status is not sufficient for inclusion.

During screening, add a note explaining whether the mortality data are attributable to the eligible HRP country, the host country, or an unclear/mixed location.
